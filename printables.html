How the printables work

Not everyone wants to solve at a screen, and we would not have it any other way. These sheets are made to be printed in plain black on white, so you can solve them with a pencil in your favorite chair, carry them on a journey, or tuck one into a card for a friend. Press the Print this page button at the foot, and the puzzles come out clean and clear, with the answer keys gathered at the end so no answer peeks at you before you are ready.

Ideas for your printed puzzles

A printed puzzle sheet is a wonderfully versatile little thing. Print a few before a long car or train journey and you will never be stuck for something calm to do. Keep a stack in a drawer for a rainy afternoon, or for the grandchildren when they visit and the weather turns. A sheet of anagrams folded into a get-well card brings a smile to someone laid up, and a missing-vowels puzzle is a gentle, screen-free way to pass a waiting-room hour.

They make thoughtful company for a friend who finds screens tiring, too. Not everyone is comfortable at a computer, and a printed sheet asks nothing of batteries, signal, or settings — only a pencil and a quiet moment. If you know someone who would enjoy a puzzle but not a website, print a page of these and pop it in the post. It is a small, kind, old-fashioned gift, and those are often the best kind.

Making your own sheets

Once you have the hang of it, making your own puzzle sheets is an easy and pleasant project. For anagrams, simply write out a list of words on a theme — a grandchild's favorite animals, the names of the family, the towns you have lived in — and jumble the letters of each. For a missing-vowels sheet, write the words and rub out the vowels. For number sequences, pick a simple rule and leave off the last number. Write the answers on the back, and you have a personal puzzle made with love that no shop could ever sell.

Away from the screen entirely

Not everyone is comfortable at a computer, and not every moment is suited to one. A printed sheet asks for none of it — no signal, no charging, no settings to fiddle with, no glare on a bright day. It works on a park bench, in a garden chair, on a train with no reception, or by lamplight in the evening when the screens have been put away. For a true escape from the hum of technology, nothing beats a pencil, a sheet of puzzles, and a quiet corner.

This matters especially for anyone who finds screens tiring or simply prefers the old ways, and there is no shame at all in that preference. A printed puzzle is not a lesser thing than an on-screen one; for many people it is the better one. We offer both because people are different, and a good puzzle page should meet you wherever you are most comfortable — whether that is at a keyboard or in your favorite armchair with a freshly sharpened pencil.

The quiet pleasure of pencil and paper

There is something a screen can never quite give you, and that is the feel of a pencil on paper. For many of us it is the way we first learned to do a puzzle, and the habit runs deep: the small weight of the pencil, the soft scratch of the lead, the satisfaction of a word written firmly in its place. A printed puzzle brings all of that back, and asks nothing of batteries, brightness settings, or a steady internet signal.

Paper is kind to the eyes, too. A printed sheet does not glow or flicker, it never times out, and it sits still on the table exactly where you left it. For a long session, or for tired eyes at the end of the day, many people simply find paper more comfortable than any screen. That is why we offer these sheets alongside our on-screen games: not as a lesser option, but as a genuinely lovely way to puzzle in its own right, and sometimes the better one.

Puzzles that travel and give

A printed puzzle sheet is one of the most portable pleasures there is. Fold a few into your bag before a long train journey, a car trip, or a flight, and you will never be short of something calm and absorbing to do. Keep a small stack in a kitchen drawer for a rainy afternoon, or for the grandchildren when they visit and the weather turns them indoors. They need no setting up and no clearing away — just a pencil and a quiet moment.

They make thoughtful little gifts, as well. A sheet of anagrams tucked into a get-well card brightens a dull day of recovery. A page of puzzles posted to a friend who finds computers tiring is a small, kind gesture that says you thought of them. And a hand-made sheet for a grandchild — their name hidden among the letters, their favorite animals waiting to be unscrambled — is the sort of gift that is kept long after the store-bought ones are forgotten. The best presents are often the ones that cost only a little time and care.

A note on comfortable reading

When you print these sheets, your browser's print settings let you choose the size, and we warmly encourage you to print them large if that suits you better. There is no virtue at all in squinting. Most browsers offer a simple way to scale a printout up, and a puzzle in generous, bold type is a far more pleasant thing to solve than one crammed into small print. Print a test page, see how it feels in your hand, and adjust until it is comfortable.

You can also use the Text size buttons at the top of the page before you print, which enlarge everything on screen and often carry through to the printed page as well. Between the two, you should be able to produce a sheet that is easy on the eyes and a joy to work through. Comfort first, always; the puzzle can only be a pleasure if the reading of it is.

Printing them nice and clear

Getting a clean, readable printout is simple. When you press Print, your browser shows a preview with a few options — chief among them the paper size and the scale. If the puzzles look small, nudge the scale up to 110 or 125 percent and watch them grow comfortably large on the page. You can also choose to print in plain black and white, which saves ink and keeps the letters crisp, and our pages are designed to look their best exactly that way.

If anything other than the puzzle sneaks onto your printout, do not worry — our print styling is set to hide the menus, buttons, and footer automatically, leaving only the puzzles and their answer key. What comes out of the printer should be a tidy sheet ready for the pencil, with nothing to distract from the fun. A moment spent in the print preview is well rewarded with a sheet that is a genuine pleasure to hold and to solve.

Keeping a puzzle box

Here is a small idea some of our readers have taken to: keep a puzzle box. Print a variety of these sheets over time, slip them into a folder or an old biscuit tin, and you will always have a ready supply to reach for — or to offer a visitor. Mix the anagrams with the missing vowels and the number sequences so there is something for every mood and every age, and add a few of your own home-made sheets as you get the knack of it.

A well-stocked puzzle box is a wonderful thing to have by the armchair, in the car, or in the bag you take to appointments. It turns an idle wait into a pleasant one, gives restless grandchildren something to settle to, and costs nothing but a little paper and the time to print. However you use them, we hope these sheets carry a little of the nook's warmth out into the world with you. When you are back at the screen, the word games and decade trivia are waiting with plenty more.
