Today's Puzzle

By the Teacup Puzzlers Editorial Team · Last reviewed: October 2026

A small, fresh pair of puzzles each day — one anagram and one missing-letter word — just enough to go with a single cup of tea.

How today's little mix works

Each day, Teacup Puzzlers sets out a small, fresh pair of puzzles — one anagram to unscramble and one missing-letter word to complete. They change on their own with the date, so tomorrow brings a new pair. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to download; just come back whenever you fancy a quick stretch for the mind.

Type your answer into the box and press Check to see how you did, or Reveal if you would rather simply be told. Neither puzzle takes more than a minute or two, which makes them the perfect companion for a single cup of tea or the quiet moment after breakfast. And if a particular day's pair proves tricky, there is always tomorrow's, along with the whole nook of games waiting on the other pages whenever you want more to do.

Why we keep it small

There is a particular charm in a puzzle you can finish in a minute. A big grid can be daunting on a tired day, but a single anagram feels like nothing at all — and yet it delivers the same satisfying click when the answer lands. We designed Today's Puzzle for exactly those moments when you want a small, certain pleasure rather than a long campaign.

It is also a lovely little ritual to keep. Many of our visitors tell us they enjoy pairing the daily pair with their first cup of the morning, a gentle way to wake the mind before the day gets going. Others save it for the afternoon lull. There is no right time and no wrong one; the puzzles will wait for you as patiently as a good friend.

The quiet power of a small win

There is a reason a tiny puzzle can lift a whole morning. A small win — any small win — gives the day a gentle push in the right direction. Solving today's anagram before breakfast is a modest thing, but it is a thing accomplished, a little proof that the mind is sharp and the day has begun well. Those small, certain successes add up, and they cost nothing but a minute or two of pleasant effort.

That is really the whole idea behind keeping the daily puzzle small. A grand challenge can be satisfying, but it can also be daunting, and on a tired day it may go untouched. A single anagram and a single missing letter ask so little that there is almost never a reason to skip them — and because they are easy to begin, they are easy to finish, which means the small win is almost guaranteed. We would rather hand you a reliable little pleasure each day than an occasional grand one, because the little ones are the ones you will actually enjoy, morning after morning.

When one cup isn't enough

If today's pair leaves you wanting more, the whole nook is open. The Word Games page holds a generous helping of ladders, anagrams, and missing-letter puzzles to work through at leisure. The Trivia by Decade rounds reward a good memory, and the Logic & Riddles page offers a cleverer sort of challenge for an evening by the fire.

And when you would like to step away from the screen altogether, our Printables let you carry a sheet of puzzles to the armchair, the porch, or a long journey. However you like to puzzle, there is a corner of the nook for you.

A gentle note on taking it easy

We will say it as often as it takes: there is no score here, no clock, and no one watching. If today's anagram will not come, leave it and try the missing letter instead, or simply press Reveal and enjoy knowing the answer. A puzzle is meant to be a pleasure, and a pleasure it should remain. Come as often or as rarely as you like, and never let a stubborn clue spoil a quiet morning.

Two gentle puzzles, two kinds of thinking

We pair an anagram with a missing-letter puzzle each day quite deliberately, because the two ask different small favors of the mind and together they make a rounded little workout. The anagram is a puzzle of arrangement: you hold a jumble of loose letters in your head and shuffle them, patiently, until they click into a word you know. It rewards a kind of mental juggling, the quiet turning-over of possibilities until one of them fits.

The missing-letter puzzle works the other way around. Here the word is almost whole, and your task is one of recognition — to see, from a clue and a single gap, the familiar word that is nearly there already. It is the gentler of the two, a matter of recognizing rather than rearranging, and it makes a lovely warm-up or a soft landing after the anagram. Between them, the daily pair touches both halves of the word-puzzler's art, and does so in a handful of unhurried minutes. You may find you prefer one to the other, and that is perfectly natural; do whichever you please first, or skip straight to your favorite. The pair is offered as a gift, not a duty, and there is no wrong way to unwrap it.

The long tradition of the daily puzzle

The daily puzzle is a tradition almost as old as the daily newspaper itself. For well over a century, readers have turned to a particular page each morning for their crossword, their word puzzle, or their quiz, as much a part of the day's routine as the first cup of coffee. There is a reason the habit has endured so long: a small daily puzzle offers something rare and valuable — a task that is complete, winnable, and finished within minutes, in a world where so much else stretches on without end.

Our daily pair carries that tradition forward in a gentle, modern form. Where once you needed a newspaper delivered to the door, now the puzzle refreshes itself quietly with the date, ready whenever you arrive. But the spirit is exactly the same: a familiar page, a fresh little challenge, and the quiet satisfaction of a thing begun and finished before the morning is out. It is a small ceremony, and small ceremonies are among the things that give a day its shape.

A ritual for any part of the day

Although we often speak of the morning, there is no rule about when to enjoy your daily puzzle. Some readers greet the day with it, a pleasant way to wake the mind before the demands of the day arrive. Others save it for the mid-morning pause, the quiet after lunch, or the settling hour of the evening when the chores are done and the chair is finally free. The puzzle does not mind when you come; it simply waits.

What matters is the gentle regularity of it, if you care for regularity at all. A small daily habit, freely chosen and freely dropped, can be a quiet comfort — a familiar visitor that asks nothing and gives a little lift each time. For those whose days have grown quieter, or who live alone, that dependable small pleasure can mean more than its size suggests. And if routine is not your way, that is perfectly fine too: dip in whenever the fancy takes you, and the puzzle will be none the worse for the wait.

Lovely with company

A daily puzzle is a fine solitary pleasure, but it is twice as nice shared. Read today's anagram aloud at the breakfast table and let whoever is with you call out a guess. Telephone a far-away friend or grandchild and race to solve the pair together over the line. Two minds often crack an anagram far faster than one, and the small shared triumph is a warm way to begin a conversation.

These little puzzles are especially good company across the generations. A grandchild will delight in beating a grandparent to the answer, and a grandparent will delight in letting them. Because the puzzles are short and the rules are plain, nobody is left out and nobody waits long for a turn. A daily puzzle shared becomes less a game than a small, recurring excuse to be in touch — and those are worth keeping.

The many shapes of a word puzzle

We alternate between anagrams and missing letters in the daily mix because a little variety keeps the mind nimble and the habit fresh. An anagram asks you to hold a jumble of letters in mind and shuffle them into sense — a different sort of thinking from the missing-letter puzzle, which asks you to recognize a familiar word from a single gap. Neither is harder than the other, exactly; they simply ask different small favors of your attention, and switching between them is part of the pleasure.

If the daily pair whets your appetite for a particular kind of puzzle, the rest of the nook is only a tab away. The Word Games page offers a generous helping of both anagrams and missing letters, plus the gentle climb of a word ladder. When you would rather test your memory than your wits, Trivia by Decade awaits, and the riddles are there for a slower, cleverer evening. There is always another cup to pour.
